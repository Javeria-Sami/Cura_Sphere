5.214 MODULE PURPOSE
The Radiology and Medical Imaging Management System shall manage the operational and clinical workflow associated with diagnostic imaging orders, imaging procedure selection, scheduling, patient arrival, imaging worklists, examination performance, imaging study tracking, radiology interpretation, report drafting, report verification, critical finding communication, final report generation, and authorized Electronic Medical Record availability.
The Radiology module shall support the following high-level workflow:
CLINICAL ENCOUNTER        │        ▼IMAGING ORDER        │        ▼ORDER VALIDATION        │        ▼IMAGING PROCEDURE        │        ▼CLINICAL / SAFETY INFORMATION        │        ▼SCHEDULING OR URGENT WORKFLOW        │        ▼PATIENT ARRIVAL        │        ▼PATIENT IDENTITY VERIFICATION        │        ▼IMAGING WORKLIST        │        ▼EXAMINATION PREPARATION        │        ▼EXAMINATION PERFORMED        │        ▼IMAGING STUDY        │        ▼RADIOLOGIST INTERPRETATION        │        ▼DRAFT REPORT        │        ▼REPORT REVIEW / VERIFICATION        │        ├── CRITICAL FINDING WORKFLOW        │        ▼FINAL RADIOLOGY REPORT        │        ▼EMR AVAILABILITY
The module shall provide traceability from the original clinical imaging request to the final verified radiology report.
Conceptually:
WHO ORDERED THE EXAMINATION?        │        ▼FOR WHICH PATIENT?        │        ▼DURING WHICH CLINICAL CONTEXT?        │        ▼WHAT IMAGING PROCEDURE WAS REQUESTED?        │        ▼WHY WAS THE EXAMINATION REQUESTED?        │        ▼WHEN WAS THE PATIENT SCHEDULED?        │        ▼WAS PATIENT IDENTITY VERIFIED?        │        ▼WHICH EXAMINATION WAS PERFORMED?        │        ▼WHICH IMAGING STUDY WAS CREATED?        │        ▼WHO INTERPRETED THE STUDY?        │        ▼WHO VERIFIED THE REPORT?        │        ▼WERE CRITICAL FINDINGS COMMUNICATED?        │        ▼WHEN DID THE REPORT BECOME FINAL?
This traceability shall be considered a fundamental radiology requirement.

5.215 RADIOLOGY DOMAIN BOUNDARY
The Radiology module shall manage diagnostic imaging workflow.
It shall not own the complete clinical encounter.
The conceptual domain relationship is:
OPD / IPD / EMERGENCY          │          ▼     IMAGING ORDER          │          ▼       RADIOLOGY          │          ├── Procedure Catalog          ├── Order Validation          ├── Scheduling          ├── Patient Arrival          ├── Safety Workflow          ├── Imaging Worklist          ├── Examination          ├── Imaging Study          ├── Interpretation          ├── Report Verification          └── Critical Findings          │          ▼          EMR
Responsibilities shall conceptually be divided as follows:
CLINICAL MODULEDoctor decides:"The patient requiresa chest X-ray."
RADIOLOGYRadiology manages:"When and how is the imagingexamination operationally performed,tracked, interpreted, and reported?"
EMRClinical record provides:"Authorized access to the finalizedradiology report in the patient'slongitudinal clinical record."
The Radiology module shall not be designed as a simple:
patient_idimagereport
table.
Real diagnostic imaging contains multiple operational, technical, and clinical states.

5.216 CRITICAL RADIOLOGY ARCHITECTURE CONCEPT
CuraSphere shall distinguish the following concepts:
IMAGING ORDER
ORDERED IMAGING PROCEDURE
RADIOLOGY APPOINTMENT
IMAGING EXAMINATION
IMAGING STUDY
RADIOLOGY REPORT
CRITICAL FINDING COMMUNICATION
These concepts shall not automatically be treated as identical.
Conceptually:
IMAGING ORDER      │      ▼WHAT WAS CLINICALLY REQUESTED?
ORDERED PROCEDURE      │      ▼WHICH IMAGING PROCEDUREWAS REQUESTED?
RADIOLOGY APPOINTMENT      │      ▼WHEN IS THE PATIENT EXPECTED?
IMAGING EXAMINATION      │      ▼WHAT PROCEDURE WASACTUALLY PERFORMED?
IMAGING STUDY      │      ▼WHAT DIAGNOSTIC IMAGE DATAWAS PRODUCED?
RADIOLOGY REPORT      │      ▼WHAT CLINICAL INTERPRETATIONWAS DOCUMENTED?
Example:
IMAGING ORDERRAD-ORD-1001Requested Procedure:CT Chest
The patient may be scheduled for:
RADIOLOGY APPOINTMENT15 JUL 202610:00 AM
After patient arrival and preparation:
IMAGING EXAMINATIONCT Chest Performed
The modality may produce:
IMAGING STUDYSTUDY-5001
The radiologist then creates:
RADIOLOGY REPORTFindingsImpression
Therefore:
ORDER = APPOINTMENT = EXAMINATION = STUDY = REPORT
is an incorrect architectural assumption.

5.219 IMAGING PROCEDURE CATALOG
CS-RAD-FR-001 — Maintain Imaging Procedure Catalog
Requirement Name
Maintain Imaging Procedure Catalog
Description
The system shall maintain a controlled catalog of imaging procedures available within CuraSphere.
Priority
Must Have
Purpose
Clinical users shall select approved imaging procedures from a controlled catalog.
Weak design:
Doctor enters:"Xray""x-ray chest""Chest xray""CXR""x ray of chest"
The system may incorrectly treat these as unrelated procedures.
A controlled procedure catalog provides:
PROCEDURE CODERAD-XR-CHEST-PA
PROCEDURE NAMEChest X-Ray PA View
Conceptual Procedure Definition
IMAGING PROCEDURE        │        ├── Procedure Code        ├── Procedure Name        ├── Modality        ├── Body Region        ├── Preparation Requirements        ├── Safety Requirements        ├── Scheduling Information        ├── Orderable Status        └── Active Status
Example
Procedure CodeRAD-CT-CHESTProcedure NameCT ChestModalityCTBody RegionChestStatusACTIVE
Business Rules
CS-RAD-BR-001
Each imaging procedure shall have a unique internal identity.
CS-RAD-BR-002
Procedure codes shall be unique within the applicable coding scope.
CS-RAD-BR-003
Only eligible active procedures shall normally be available for new imaging orders.
CS-RAD-BR-004
Historical imaging records shall preserve applicable procedure relationships.

5.270 RADIOLOGY MODULE RISK ANALYSIS
Risk 1 — Wrong Patient Imaging Study
Problem
An imaging study belonging to Patient A is associated with Patient B.
Impact
Severe patient safety risk.
A doctor may make clinical decisions based on another patient’s imaging.
Mitigation
Validate:
PATIENT   │   ▼IMAGING ORDER   │   ▼EXAMINATION   │   ▼IMAGING STUDY
relationships.
Patient-study association shall be treated as safety-critical.

Risk 2 — Patient Name Used as Unique Imaging Identifier
Problem
The system relies on:
Ahmad Khan
to identify imaging work.
Impact
Patients with identical names may be confused.
Mitigation
Use validated patient and imaging workflow identifiers.

Risk 3 — Appointment and Examination Treated as Same Record
Problem
The database assumes:
APPOINTMENT = EXAMINATION
Impact
The system cannot correctly represent:
no-show;
late arrival;
rescheduling;
examination not performed;
actual start time;
actual completion time.
Mitigation
Model appointment and examination as distinct domain concepts.

Risk 4 — Safety Questions Hardcoded in React
Problem
MRI safety questions exist only inside:
MRIForm.jsx
Impact
Clinical safety workflow becomes frontend-dependent and difficult to govern.
Mitigation
Use approved safety requirement configuration and trusted backend validation.

Risk 5 — Incomplete Safety Workflow Bypassed
Problem
A user manually calls:
POST /api/radiology/examinations/start
without completing required screening.
Impact
UI controls can be bypassed.
Mitigation
The ASP.NET Core backend shall validate required workflow state before starting the examination.

Risk 6 — Original Imaging Order Silently Changed
Problem
The ordered procedure is changed after the examination so the order appears to match performed work.
Impact
Clinical ordering history becomes inaccurate.
Mitigation
Preserve:
ORDERED PROCEDURE
and separately record:
PERFORMED PROCEDURE
where required.

Risk 7 — Diagnostic Images Stored as Random Application Uploads
Problem
Developers build:
/uploads/xray1.jpg/uploads/xray2.jpg
as the complete medical imaging architecture.
Impact
Poor imaging interoperability, traceability, metadata management, and clinical workflow support.
Mitigation
Design a formal imaging integration boundary and evaluate PACS/DICOM architecture.

Risk 8 — Draft Report Appears in EMR as Final
Problem
A radiology resident saves a draft.
The EMR displays it as a verified report.
Impact
Clinical decisions may be based on incomplete interpretation.
Mitigation
Respect report state and approved release rules.

Risk 9 — Final Report Silently Edited
Problem
A finalized impression is changed using an ordinary database update.
Impact
Clinical report integrity and traceability are lost.
Mitigation
Use controlled correction or addendum workflows.

Risk 10 — Critical Finding Only Shown as Red Badge
Problem
The UI displays:
CRITICAL
but no communication record exists.
Impact
The urgent finding may never reach the responsible clinical team.
Mitigation
Implement critical finding communication and acknowledgement workflow.

Risk 11 — Radiologist Report Not Linked to Study
Problem
The report only contains:
patient_idreport_text
Impact
The system cannot reliably determine which imaging study was interpreted.
Mitigation
Maintain explicit report-to-imaging-work relationships.

Risk 12 — One Date Field for Radiology Workflow
Problem
Database contains:
radiology_date
Impact
The system cannot distinguish:
order time;
scheduled time;
arrival time;
examination start;
examination completion;
report creation;
report verification.
Mitigation
Record meaningful workflow event timestamps.

Risk 13 — React Controls Report Authorization
Problem
Frontend logic:
if (user.role === "Radiologist") {    showVerifyButton();}
is treated as the security model.
Impact
API requests may bypass the hidden button.
Mitigation
The ASP.NET Core backend shall perform authoritative permission and workflow validation.

Risk 14 — PACS Vendor Logic Embedded in Core Domain
Problem
Core radiology code directly depends on one vendor-specific API throughout the application.
Impact
Vendor replacement becomes expensive and risky.
Mitigation
Use an integration abstraction:
RADIOLOGY DOMAIN       │       ▼IMAGING INTEGRATION CONTRACT       │       ▼PACS ADAPTER       │       ▼VENDOR SYSTEM
