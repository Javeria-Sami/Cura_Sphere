6.21 Compliance, Standards & Quality Attributes

6.21.1 Introduction
The Compliance, Standards, and Quality Attributes Requirements define the regulatory, legal, ethical, technical, and operational standards that govern the design, development, deployment, operation, and maintenance of the CuraSphere Hospital Management System.
Healthcare information systems manage highly confidential patient information, financial records, laboratory results, prescriptions, diagnostic images, and operational data. Consequently, the system shall comply with internationally recognized healthcare standards, software engineering standards, cybersecurity frameworks, accessibility guidelines, and applicable legal regulations while maintaining high levels of software quality throughout its lifecycle.
These requirements establish the baseline for ensuring that CuraSphere is reliable, secure, interoperable, maintainable, auditable, and suitable for deployment in hospitals of varying sizes.

6.21.19 Section Summary
The Compliance, Standards, and Quality Attributes framework establishes the policies and controls required to ensure that CuraSphere is developed and operated according to recognized healthcare, information security, and software engineering practices. By integrating governance, risk management, quality assurance, and continuous improvement into the software lifecycle, the system is designed to support secure, reliable, and maintainable healthcare operations while remaining adaptable to evolving regulatory and organizational requirements.

6.23 Section 6 Final Summary
Section 6 defines the complete Non-Functional Requirements for the CuraSphere Hospital Management System. These requirements complement the functional capabilities described in Section 5 by specifying the quality attributes, operational expectations, and technical constraints necessary for a secure, reliable, scalable, and maintainable enterprise healthcare platform.
The completed non-functional requirements include:

Consolidated Traceability Matrix
Together, these requirements establish measurable criteria for system quality, operational resilience, regulatory readiness, and long-term maintainability. They also provide the foundation for the subsequent architectural design, database design, implementation, testing, deployment, and operational support activities described in later sections of the SRS.
