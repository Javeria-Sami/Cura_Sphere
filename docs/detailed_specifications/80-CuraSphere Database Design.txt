8.1 Database Design

8.1.1 Introduction
The Database Design defines the logical and physical structure of the data repository used by the CuraSphere Hospital Management System. It specifies how healthcare information is organized, stored, protected, retrieved, and maintained throughout its lifecycle.
The database serves as the central repository for all operational, clinical, financial, administrative, and audit data generated by the hospital. Because CuraSphere manages sensitive patient records and mission-critical workflows, the database architecture has been designed to provide:
High availability
ACID-compliant transactions
Strong data integrity
Scalability
Security
Fault tolerance
Regulatory compliance
Efficient querying
Disaster recovery support
The database shall support both transactional workloads (OLTP) and reporting workloads while maintaining data consistency and performance.

8.1.2 Purpose
The purpose of the database design is to:
Provide centralized data storage.
Ensure data integrity and consistency.
Support all business modules.
Maintain relationships between entities.
Minimize data redundancy.
Improve query performance.
Protect confidential healthcare data.
Support reporting and analytics.
Enable backup and disaster recovery.
Ensure future scalability.

8.1.3 Objectives
The database architecture shall:
Store complete hospital information securely.
Support millions of patient records.
Process thousands of concurrent transactions.
Ensure referential integrity.
Support multi-hospital deployments.
Allow horizontal application scaling.
Maintain historical records.
Support regulatory auditing.
Facilitate disaster recovery.
Optimize database performance.

8.1.7 Database Design Principles
The database shall follow the following design principles:
Principle 1 — Data Integrity
All stored data shall be:
Accurate
Complete
Consistent
Valid
Reliable

Principle 2 — Normalization
Database tables shall generally conform to the Third Normal Form (3NF) to reduce redundancy and update anomalies. Controlled denormalization may be introduced for reporting or performance-critical scenarios, provided data consistency is maintained.

Principle 3 — Referential Integrity
Relationships between entities shall be enforced through:
Primary Keys
Foreign Keys
Unique Constraints
Check Constraints
This ensures invalid references cannot exist.

Principle 4 — Security by Design
Sensitive data shall be protected using:
Encryption at rest (where applicable)
Encryption in transit (TLS)
Role-based database permissions
Least-privilege access
Audit logging

Principle 5 — Scalability
The database shall support:
Large patient populations
Multi-year medical histories
Increasing transaction volumes
Multi-hospital deployments
Future functional expansion

Principle 6 — Availability
The database architecture shall provide:
Replication
Automated backups
Point-in-time recovery
Failover capability
Health monitoring

8.2 Conceptual Data Model

8.2.1 Introduction
The Conceptual Data Model (CDM) provides a high-level representation of the core business entities within the CuraSphere Hospital Management System and the relationships between them. It focuses on business concepts rather than implementation details, serving as the bridge between business requirements and the logical database design.
The conceptual model is independent of any specific database technology and enables stakeholders—including hospital administrators, clinicians, software architects, database designers, and developers—to understand how hospital information is organized and related.
This model establishes the foundation for the Logical Data Model (Section 8.3) and the Physical Data Model (Section 8.4).

8.2.2 Objectives
The Conceptual Data Model shall:
Identify all major business entities.
Define relationships between entities.
Eliminate ambiguity in business terminology.
Support all functional modules of CuraSphere.
Serve as the foundation for logical and physical database design.
Ensure complete coverage of business processes.
Promote data consistency across the system.

8.3 Logical Data Model

8.3.1 Introduction
The Logical Data Model (LDM) transforms the business-oriented Conceptual Data Model into a structured representation suitable for relational database implementation. It defines the logical entities, attributes, relationships, primary keys, foreign keys, business constraints, and normalization rules while remaining independent of physical storage implementation.
The Logical Data Model ensures that all business processes identified in previous sections are supported by a consistent and normalized data structure.
Unlike the Conceptual Data Model, which focuses on business objects, the Logical Data Model specifies:
Logical entities
Entity attributes
Primary Keys (PK)
Foreign Keys (FK)
Cardinality
Mandatory and optional relationships
Business constraints
Data domains
Normalization rules

8.3.2 Objectives
The Logical Data Model shall:
Define all logical entities.
Establish complete relationships.
Eliminate data redundancy.
Maintain referential integrity.
Support all hospital workflows.
Enable scalable database implementation.
Ensure consistency between functional modules.
Provide a blueprint for physical database design.

8.3.14 Business Constraints
The logical model shall enforce the following constraints:
Constraint
Description
Unique Patient Identifier
Every patient must have a unique identifier
One Active Admission
A patient cannot have multiple active admissions simultaneously unless organizational policy allows transfers.
Doctor Availability
Appointment times shall not overlap for the same doctor.
Stock Validation
Medicine quantity shall not become negative.
Invoice Integrity
Invoice totals shall equal the sum of invoice items plus applicable taxes and adjustments.
Mandatory Clinical Link
Every prescription, laboratory order, and radiology order shall reference a valid encounter.

8.3.16 Normalization Strategy
The database shall generally adhere to Third Normal Form (3NF).
First Normal Form (1NF)
Requirements:
Atomic values
No repeating groups
Unique rows

Second Normal Form (2NF)
Requirements:
Meet 1NF.
Non-key attributes fully depend on the primary key.

Third Normal Form (3NF)
Requirements:
Meet 2NF.
Eliminate transitive dependencies.
Store each fact only once.

Controlled Denormalization
Controlled denormalization may be introduced for:
Executive dashboards
Reporting warehouses
Performance-critical analytical queries
Any denormalized structures shall be documented and synchronized to preserve data consistency.

8.4 Physical Data Model & Database Schema

8.4.1 Introduction
The Physical Data Model (PDM) defines the implementation-ready database schema for the CuraSphere Hospital Management System using PostgreSQL. It translates the Logical Data Model into physical database objects, including schemas, tables, columns, data types, indexes, constraints, relationships, and storage considerations.
The physical model is optimized for:
High transaction throughput
Data integrity
Query performance
Scalability
Security
Maintainability
Disaster recovery
Future extensibility
Unlike the Logical Data Model, which focuses on entities and relationships, the Physical Data Model specifies the exact database implementation details required for development.

8.4.2 Objectives
The Physical Data Model shall:
Define the PostgreSQL database structure.
Specify physical schemas and tables.
Define column names and data types.
Establish primary and foreign key constraints.
Implement indexing strategies.
Support efficient query execution.
Ensure referential integrity.
Optimize storage and retrieval performance.

An encounter shall not be deleted once clinical documentation has been finalized. Corrections shall follow the organization's amendment policy to preserve the audit trail.

8.5.48 Business Rules
BR-DB-013
Every laboratory order shall be associated with one valid clinical encounter.
BR-DB-014
Each laboratory sample barcode shall be unique across the organization.
BR-DB-015
A radiology report shall not be finalized until the corresponding imaging study has been completed.
BR-DB-016
A prescription shall contain at least one prescription item before it can be finalized.
BR-DB-017
The dispensed quantity of a medication shall not exceed the prescribed quantity without documented authorization according to hospital policy.
BR-DB-018
Invoice totals shall be automatically calculated from invoice items, taxes, discounts, and adjustments.
BR-DB-019
A payment amount shall not exceed the outstanding invoice balance unless overpayments are explicitly supported and tracked by organizational policy.
BR-DB-020
Insurance claims shall reference both a valid invoice and an active patient insurance policy.

8.5.49 Acceptance Criteria
The Laboratory, Radiology, Pharmacy, Billing, and Financial data dictionary shall be accepted when:
Laboratory workflow tables are fully specified.
Radiology order and reporting tables are defined.
Prescription and medication dispensing structures support medication management.
Invoice, invoice item, payment, and insurance claim tables support the complete billing lifecycle.
Primary keys, foreign keys, constraints, indexes, and business rules are documented.
The schema maintains referential integrity across all diagnostic and financial modules.
