Part 14.1 - Database Design Overview

14.1 Database Design Overview

14.1.1 Introduction
The Database Design Specification defines the logical and physical database structure for the CuraSphere Hospital Management System (HMS). It provides the blueprint for implementing the PostgreSQL database that supports all clinical, administrative, financial, operational, and analytical functions of the system.
The database has been designed to ensure:
High data integrity
Enterprise scalability
Regulatory compliance
High performance
Security
Maintainability
Data consistency
Future extensibility
The database serves as the single source of truth for all healthcare information managed within CuraSphere.

14.1.2 Purpose
The purpose of the database design is to:
Store hospital information securely.
Support all hospital business processes.
Maintain relationships among healthcare entities.
Prevent data redundancy.
Improve query performance.
Support auditing and compliance.
Facilitate reporting and analytics.
Enable disaster recovery.
Support integration with external healthcare systems.
Ensure long-term maintainability.

14.1.5 Database Design Principles
The database shall adhere to the following design principles.
A. Data Integrity
Every stored record shall remain complete, valid, and internally consistent.

B. Normalization
Tables shall be normalized to reduce redundancy while maintaining acceptable performance.

C. Security by Design
Sensitive healthcare information shall be protected through multiple security mechanisms.

D. Scalability
The database shall support future organizational growth without requiring significant redesign.

E. Modularity
Business domains shall be logically separated to simplify maintenance and future expansion.

F. Performance Optimization
Indexes, query optimization, partitioning, and caching shall be considered during implementation.

G. Auditability
Critical operations shall generate immutable audit records.

14.3.1 Introduction
The Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) defines the logical data model of the CuraSphere Hospital Management System (HMS). It illustrates the entities, attributes, relationships, and cardinality rules required to support all hospital operations.
The ERD serves as the foundation for database implementation and ensures that data is organized, normalized, and interconnected according to business requirements.
The conceptual model presented in this section shall be used as the basis for detailed physical database design in subsequent sections.

14.3.2 Objectives
The ERD shall:
Model all healthcare business entities.
Define relationships among entities.
Minimize data redundancy.
Ensure referential integrity.
Support normalization.
Simplify database implementation.
Facilitate reporting.
Support scalability.
Enable interoperability.
Improve maintainability.

14.3.3 ERD Design Principles
The ERD shall comply with the following principles:
Every entity shall represent a unique business concept.
Every entity shall possess a primary key.
Relationships shall enforce referential integrity.
Business rules shall determine relationship cardinality.
Normalization shall eliminate unnecessary redundancy.
Nullable attributes shall be minimized.
Surrogate keys shall be used where appropriate.
Audit fields shall be included in business entities.
Naming conventions defined in Section 14.2 shall be followed.

14.5.111 Security Business Rules
The Administration and Security module shall comply with the following rules:
Every authenticated user shall have at least one assigned role.
Authorization decisions shall be based on Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
Passwords shall be stored using secure hashing algorithms (e.g., Argon2 or bcrypt).
Multi-factor authentication shall be configurable for privileged accounts.
Every successful and failed login attempt shall be recorded.
Sensitive actions, including patient record updates, financial transactions, and configuration changes, shall generate audit log entries.
Audit records shall be immutable and retained according to organizational data retention policies.
Session timeouts and password policies shall be configurable through system settings.
Notifications shall support in-app, email, and SMS delivery channels.
Administrative actions shall require appropriate authorization and be fully traceable.

14.5.112 Administration Domain Acceptance Criteria
The Administration, Security & Audit Domain shall be considered complete when:
All administration-related tables are fully defined with columns, primary keys, foreign keys, constraints, and relationships.
RBAC enforces secure authentication and authorization across all system modules.
Audit logging provides complete traceability for critical business and security events.
Login history, notifications, employee records, and system configurations maintain referential integrity.
Security controls support password policies, multi-factor authentication, session management, and immutable audit trails.
The domain integrates seamlessly with every module of the CuraSphere Hospital Management System while ensuring confidentiality, integrity, availability, and regulatory compliance.
