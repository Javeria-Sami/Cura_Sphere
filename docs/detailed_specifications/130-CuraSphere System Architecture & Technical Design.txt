13.1 Architecture Overview

13.1.1 Introduction
The System Architecture defines the overall technical structure of the CuraSphere Hospital Management System (HMS). It describes how software components, hardware resources, databases, communication interfaces, external systems, and users interact to provide a secure, scalable, maintainable, and high-performance healthcare platform.
The architecture serves as the blueprint for software developers, solution architects, database administrators, DevOps engineers, QA teams, security specialists, and infrastructure administrators throughout the software development lifecycle.
CuraSphere adopts a modern enterprise architecture based on modular design principles, service-oriented communication, layered architecture, RESTful APIs, secure authentication mechanisms, and cloud-ready deployment. The architecture is designed to support hospitals ranging from small clinics to multi-campus healthcare organizations.

13.1.2 Architecture Goals
The primary goals of the CuraSphere architecture are to:
Provide a highly scalable healthcare platform.
Support modular system development.
Ensure high availability and reliability.
Enable secure communication.
Simplify maintenance and upgrades.
Support enterprise integrations.
Facilitate cloud and on-premises deployments.
Maintain regulatory compliance.
Improve development productivity.
Support future expansion without major redesign.

13.2.6 SOLID Principles
The application architecture shall follow the SOLID object-oriented design principles.
Single Responsibility Principle (SRP)
Each class or component shall have only one reason to change.
Example:
PatientService handles patient operations.
BillingService handles billing operations.
AppointmentService handles appointments.

Open/Closed Principle (OCP)
Software entities shall be open for extension but closed for modification.
New functionality shall preferably be implemented through extension rather than altering existing, stable components.

Liskov Substitution Principle (LSP)
Derived classes shall be replaceable for their base classes without affecting system behavior.
This principle promotes reliable inheritance and polymorphism.

Interface Segregation Principle (ISP)
Large interfaces shall be divided into smaller, specialized interfaces.
Example:
Instead of:
IHospitalService
Use:
IPatientService
IDoctorService
IPharmacyService

Dependency Inversion Principle (DIP)
High-level modules shall depend on abstractions rather than concrete implementations.
Example:
PatientService
      │
      ▼
IPatientRepository
      │
      ▼
PostgreSQLRepository

13.4 Layered Architecture

Each layer has a clearly defined purpose and communicates only with adjacent layers through well-defined interfaces. This separation improves maintainability, scalability, security, testing, and future extensibility.
CuraSphere adopts a five-layer enterprise architecture with additional cross-cutting services that provide logging, security, caching, monitoring, and configuration management.

13.4.2 Objectives
The Layered Architecture shall:
Separate presentation from business logic.
Isolate database access.
Reduce coupling between components.
Improve maintainability.
Enable independent testing.
Improve scalability.
Simplify future enhancements.
Improve security.
Standardize communication.
Support enterprise deployment.

13.16.19 Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity Acceptance Criteria
The Disaster Recovery & Business Continuity Architecture shall be considered complete when:
Business continuity strategies identify and prioritize critical healthcare services.
Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPO) are defined and achievable.
Backup, replication, failover, and recovery procedures are documented and regularly tested.
Disaster recovery governance, communication, and validation processes ensure coordinated restoration of services.
The architecture supports resilient, secure, and reliable healthcare operations with minimal disruption during adverse events.
