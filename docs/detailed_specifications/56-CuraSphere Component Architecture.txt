7.4 Component Architecture

7.4.1 Introduction
The Component Architecture defines the internal structure of CuraSphere by decomposing the Hospital Management System into modular, reusable, and independently deployable software components. Each component encapsulates a specific business capability and communicates with other components through standardized interfaces and service contracts.
The component-based architecture improves:
Maintainability
Scalability
Testability
Reusability
Fault Isolation
Team Collaboration
Continuous Deployment
Future Expansion
Each component owns its business logic while depending only on published interfaces rather than internal implementations.

7.4.2 Objectives
The Component Architecture shall:
Divide the HMS into independent modules.
Minimize coupling between components.
Maximize cohesion within components.
Define clear service boundaries.
Enable independent testing and deployment.
Support horizontal scaling of individual modules.
Facilitate integration with external systems.
Provide a foundation for future microservice adoption if required.

7.4.5 Component Interaction Principles
All components shall adhere to the following architectural principles:
Loose Coupling
Components shall interact through service interfaces and APIs.
High Cohesion
Each component shall implement a single business domain.
Encapsulation
Internal implementation details shall remain hidden from other components.
Interface-Based Communication
Components shall expose documented interfaces rather than internal classes.
Independent Testing
Each component shall be testable without requiring the entire system.

7.4.23 Component Design Rules
All components shall comply with the following rules:
Each component shall expose only documented service interfaces.
Components shall not directly access another component's database tables.
Shared business logic shall be implemented through reusable services.
Component dependencies shall remain acyclic.
Sensitive operations shall generate audit logs.
Components shall validate all incoming requests.
Long-running tasks shall be delegated to asynchronous background workers.
Configuration values shall be externalized rather than hardcoded.

7.4.24 Acceptance Criteria
The Component Architecture shall be accepted when:
All major business capabilities are encapsulated within dedicated components.
Component responsibilities are clearly defined and do not overlap unnecessarily.
Service interfaces are documented and used consistently.
Inter-component communication follows approved architectural patterns.
Components can be tested independently.
Cross-component dependencies are minimized and traceable.
Security, logging, and configuration requirements are applied consistently across all components.
