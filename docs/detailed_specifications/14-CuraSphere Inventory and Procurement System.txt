5.412 MODULE PURPOSE
The Inventory, Procurement and Hospital Supply Chain Management System shall manage the controlled acquisition, receipt, storage, movement, issuance, consumption, return, adjustment, and traceability of eligible hospital inventory items.
The module shall support:
item master management;
item categorization;
unit of measure management;
storage location management;
warehouse management;
department stock locations;
supplier management;
purchase requisitions;
requisition approval;
purchase orders;
purchase order approval;
goods receipt;
partial goods receipt;
excess receipt prevention;
rejected receipt quantities;
batch and lot tracking;
expiry tracking;
stock availability;
stock movement;
stock transfer;
department stock issue;
department stock receipt;
stock consumption;
stock return;
supplier return;
stock adjustment;
stock count;
stock reconciliation;
stock reservation architecture;
low-stock monitoring;
reorder configuration;
expiry monitoring;
inventory audit trail;
supply chain reporting.
The high-level supply chain workflow shall be:
ITEM REQUIREMENT      │      ▼PURCHASE REQUISITION      │      ▼APPROVAL WORKFLOW      │      ▼PURCHASE ORDER      │      ▼SUPPLIER      │      ▼GOODS DELIVERY      │      ▼GOODS RECEIPT      │      ▼QUALITY / RECEIPT VALIDATION      │      ▼BATCH / LOT REGISTRATION      │      ▼WAREHOUSE STOCK      │      ▼STOCK MOVEMENT      │      ├───────────────┐      │               │      ▼               ▼STOCK TRANSFER   DEPARTMENT ISSUE      │               │      ▼               ▼OTHER LOCATION   DEPARTMENT STOCK                      │                      ▼                  CONSUMPTION                      │               ┌──────┴──────┐               │             │               ▼             ▼             RETURN       FINAL USAGE               │               ▼         STOCK MOVEMENT
The module shall preserve traceability for:
ITEM  │  ▼PROCUREMENT SOURCE  │  ▼SUPPLIER  │  ▼PURCHASE ORDER  │  ▼GOODS RECEIPT  │  ▼BATCH / LOT  │  ▼STORAGE LOCATION  │  ▼STOCK MOVEMENT  │  ▼ISSUE / TRANSFER / CONSUMPTION

5.413 INVENTORY DOMAIN BOUNDARY
The Inventory and Procurement module shall own hospital supply chain and physical stock control.
It shall not own:
clinical diagnosis;
medication prescribing;
laboratory result interpretation;
radiology reporting;
patient billing;
general ledger accounting;
employee payroll.
Conceptually:
PROCUREMENT     │     ▼ACQUIRE ITEMS     │     ▼INVENTORY     │     ▼CONTROL PHYSICAL STOCK     │     ├────────────► PHARMACY     │     ├────────────► LABORATORY     │     ├────────────► RADIOLOGY     │     ├────────────► OPERATION THEATRE     │     ├────────────► WARDS     │     ├────────────► EMERGENCY     │     └────────────► OTHER DEPARTMENTS
The Pharmacy module may answer:
Which medicine was dispensed to the patient?
The Inventory module answers:
Which physical stock quantity and batch moved because of that dispensing event?
The Laboratory module may answer:
Which laboratory test was performed?
The Inventory module answers:
Which reagent or consumable stock was used?
The Billing module may answer:
What amount was charged?
The Inventory module answers:
What physical item quantity exists and why did it change?
Therefore:
CLINICAL EVENT      ≠STOCK MOVEMENT      ≠FINANCIAL CHARGE

5.414 CRITICAL INVENTORY ARCHITECTURE CONCEPT
CuraSphere shall distinguish:
ITEM DEFINITION
SUPPLIER
PURCHASE REQUISITION
PURCHASE ORDER
GOODS RECEIPT
BATCH / LOT
STOCK LOCATION
STOCK BALANCE
STOCK MOVEMENT
STOCK ISSUE
STOCK CONSUMPTION
STOCK RETURN
STOCK ADJUSTMENT
These concepts shall not be collapsed into one inventory table.
Weak architecture:
inventoryitem_namesupplierquantitypurchase_priceexpiry_datedepartment
This design cannot properly explain:
WHICH PURCHASE ORDER?WHICH GOODS RECEIPT?WHICH BATCH?WHICH LOCATION?WHICH MOVEMENT?WHICH ISSUE?WHICH RETURN?WHY DID QUANTITY CHANGE?
A stronger conceptual architecture is:
ITEM MASTER     │     ▼PROCUREMENT     │     ▼GOODS RECEIPT     │     ▼BATCH / LOT     │     ▼STOCK LOCATION     │     ▼STOCK MOVEMENT LEDGER     │     ▼CURRENT STOCK PROJECTION

5.417 ITEM MASTER
CS-INV-FR-001 — Maintain Item Master
Description
The system shall maintain a controlled master catalog of inventory items.
Priority
Must Have
Conceptual Item Model
ITEM │ ├── Item ID ├── Item Code ├── Item Name ├── Item Category ├── Base Unit ├── Batch Tracked ├── Expiry Tracked ├── Stock Controlled ├── Reorder Controlled └── Active Status
Example Items
ITEM CODEMED-PARA-500
ITEM NAMEParacetamol 500 mg Tablet
Another:
ITEM CODELAB-EDTA-3ML
ITEM NAMEEDTA Blood Collection Tube 3 mL
Another:
ITEM CODESURG-GLOVE-M

Business Rules
CS-INV-BR-001
Each inventory item shall have a unique internal identity.
CS-INV-BR-002
Item codes shall be unique within the applicable inventory scope.
CS-INV-BR-003
Inactive items shall not normally be available for new stock transactions.

5.420 ITEM UNIT CONFIGURATION
CS-INV-FR-008 — Maintain Item Unit Configuration
The system shall support applicable item unit configurations.
Example
ITEMParacetamol 500 mg
Possible units:
1 BOX   │   ▼10 STRIPS   │   ▼100 TABLETS
Another:
1 CARTON   │   ▼20 BOXES
Critical Architecture Problem
A beginner may store:
quantity = 10
But what does 10 mean?
10 TABLETS?10 STRIPS?10 BOXES?10 CARTONS?
The system shall maintain quantity together with applicable unit context.
The final unit conversion model shall be defined during Inventory Domain Architecture.

The system shall support First-Expire, First-Out stock selection for applicable expiry-controlled inventory.
Priority
Must Have
Example:
BATCH AEXPIRY31 DEC 2026QUANTITY500
BATCH BEXPIRY30 JUN 2027QUANTITY1,000
Issue request:
200
FEFO recommendation:
BATCH A
because:
31 DEC 2026     <30 JUN 2027
Critical Principle
FEFO is:
FIRST EXPIREFIRST OUT
It is not automatically the same as:
FIFOFIRST INFIRST OUT
For expiry-sensitive hospital inventory, FEFO may be operationally required.
The system shall support the applicable selection strategy.

5.493 INVENTORY AND PROCUREMENT RISK ANALYSIS
Risk 1 — One Inventory Table Stores Everything
Problem
itemsupplierquantityexpirydepartment
are stored in one record.
Impact
Procurement, batch, location, and movement traceability become unreliable.
Mitigation
Separate item, procurement, receipt, batch, location, and movement concepts.

Risk 2 — Quantity Changes Without Movement History
Problem
stock.quantity -= 20;
Impact
The system cannot explain why stock decreased.
Mitigation
Maintain stock movement provenance.

Risk 3 — Ordered Quantity Treated as Received Quantity
Problem
Purchase order contains 1,000 units.
Supplier delivers 700.
System adds 1,000 to stock.
Impact
Stock is overstated by 300.
Mitigation
Record actual goods receipt quantities.

Risk 4 — No Partial Goods Receipt
Problem
The system assumes one purchase order equals one delivery.
Impact
Real supplier delivery workflows cannot be represented.
Mitigation
Support one or more goods receipts against eligible purchase orders.

Risk 5 — Excess Receipt Is Not Validated
Problem
Ordered:
1,000
Received:
1,500
System accepts automatically.
Impact
Procurement and stock inconsistency.
Mitigation
Validate cumulative receipt quantity.

Risk 6 — No Batch Tracking
Problem
All medication stock is stored as:
Paracetamol = 5,000
Impact
Expiry and recall traceability become impossible.
Mitigation
Maintain applicable batch or lot identity.

Risk 7 — Current Stock Includes Expired Batches
Problem
Dashboard displays:
AVAILABLE = 5,000
but:
3,000
are expired.
Impact
Users believe usable stock is available.
Mitigation
Separate usable and non-usable stock context.

Risk 8 — FIFO Used Where FEFO Is Required
Problem
Oldest received batch is issued first even though another batch expires sooner.
Impact
Avoidable expiry and waste.
Mitigation
Support FEFO selection for applicable items.

Risk 9 — Negative Stock Race Condition
Problem
Two users simultaneously issue the same stock.
Impact
Stock becomes negative.
Mitigation
Use database-level concurrency control and transactional validation.

Risk 10 — Transfer Immediately Adds Destination Stock
Problem
Stock is dispatched but not physically received.
Destination already sees stock as available.
Impact
The destination may issue stock it does not possess.
Mitigation
Represent transfer and in-transit workflow.

Risk 11 — Stock Consumption Duplicated by Integration Retry
Problem
Pharmacy sends the same dispensing event twice.
Impact
Stock decreases twice.
Mitigation
Use source event identity and idempotency.

Risk 12 — Stock Return Exceeds Original Issue
Problem
Issued:
100
Returned:
150
Impact
Stock is artificially increased.
Mitigation
Validate returnable quantity.

Risk 13 — Direct Stock Adjustment
Problem
User directly changes:
quantity = 500
Impact
Inventory history disappears.
Mitigation
Use controlled stock adjustment events.

Risk 14 — Physical Count Automatically Overwrites Stock
Problem
A counting mistake immediately changes authoritative stock.
Impact
Inventory becomes incorrect.
Mitigation
Separate count, variance review, and reconciliation.

Risk 15 — Approval Controlled Only by React
Problem
Approval button is hidden from ordinary users.
A user directly calls:
POST /api/purchase-orders/291/approve
Impact
Unauthorized procurement approval.
Mitigation
ASP.NET Core backend authorization policies.

Risk 16 — Requisition and Purchase Order Are the Same
Problem
Department requests automatically become supplier orders.
Impact
Procurement control is bypassed.
Mitigation
Separate internal requirement from external procurement commitment.

Risk 17 — No Supplier Historical Traceability
Problem
Supplier record is overwritten or deleted.
Impact
Historical purchase orders lose procurement provenance.
Mitigation
Preserve supplier identity and historical relationships.

Risk 18 — Low Stock Uses Hospital-Wide Total Only
Problem
Central warehouse has sufficient stock.
Emergency department has zero stock.
Dashboard reports:
STOCK SUFFICIENT
Impact
Operational shortages are hidden.
Mitigation
Support location-specific reorder and stock monitoring.
